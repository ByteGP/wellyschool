### D020 - Inherited guilt and original sin

- **Decision status:** Adopted
- **Workflow disposition:** Active governance
- **Project-owner decision:** Approved with project wording
- **Decision date:** 2026-08-12
- **Decision note:** Teach that morally accountable persons sin and deserve death; children and persons lacking capacity are not accountable until capable of recognizing sin.
- **Claim classification:** `framework_position`
- **Scope:** Secondary disputed
- **Framework position:** All morally accountable people sin and therefore deserve death. Accountability presupposes the capacity to recognize sin and make a meaningful moral response; infants, children who have not reached that capacity, and persons whose mental capacity prevents such recognition are not held personally accountable for sin they cannot understand.
- **Key passages:** Romans 3:23; 6:23; Ezekiel 18:20; James 4:17; Deuteronomy 1:39
- **Strongest credible alternative:** Augustinian and Reformed accounts teach inherited guilt or condemnation from Adam, while other traditions distinguish inherited mortality or corruption from personal guilt.
- **Interpretive hinge:** The relationship between Adamic death, inherited consequences, personal guilt, knowledge, capacity, and individual responsibility.
- **First introduction / full treatment:** Kids / Teens
- **Child-facing rule:** Teach sin as a personally accountable choice once a person can understand right, wrong, and repentance; never tell a young child or person lacking capacity that they are personally condemned.
- **Prohibited overstatement:** Do not assign one fixed chronological age of accountability to every person or teach that infants and people lacking relevant capacity bear personal guilt.
- **Targeted research or protocol need:** Passage-level packet on Romans 5, Ezekiel 18, moral knowledge, and accountability; develop a capacity-sensitive pastoral explanation rather than a fixed-age rule.
- **Owner:** Project owner / appointed reviewers
