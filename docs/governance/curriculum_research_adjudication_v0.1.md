# Curriculum Research Adjudication and Canonical Architecture v0.1

**Project:** Scripture-centred Sunday School Teacher and Family Web System  
**Status:** Working decision document  
**Inputs:** Six independent curriculum-research outputs: two each for Kids, Youths, and Teens  
**Purpose:** Establish the curriculum and content-system foundation that must exist before producing the comprehensive Claude Code implementation brief.

---

## 1. Executive Decision

The six research files are useful as **curriculum proposals and coverage inventories**, but they are not reliable enough to become the canonical curriculum by direct merger or majority vote.

The adjudicated direction is:

1. Use the more cautious, non-Gemini output as the principal **developmental and epistemic baseline** for each segment.
2. Salvage the Gemini outputs' stronger thematic labels, broader issue coverage, and some useful lesson ideas.
3. Reject unsupported certainty, unsupplied doctrinal positions, and claims presented as historical or scientific proof without adequate evidence.
4. Replace the age-locked, once-through sequence in all six files with a **rotation-safe spiral curriculum** that works for mixed-age segment classes.
5. Publish **30 lesson units per annual cycle**, but schedule only 24–28 as normal teaching Sundays. The remaining units are flex, review, holiday, catch-up, or optional family-study content.
6. Build one canonical lesson object that produces two experiences: **Teacher** and **Family**.
7. Store theological assertions with an explicit evidence classification so that the CMS never treats a framework conclusion as though it were a direct quotation from Scripture.
8. Keep published theology human-reviewed. AI may assist research and drafting outside the product, but the live web application should retrieve approved content rather than generate doctrine at runtime.

This produces a proposed corpus of:

- **Kids:** 5 rotating annual cycles x 30 units = 150 lesson units
- **Youths:** 4 rotating annual cycles x 30 units = 120 lesson units
- **Teens:** 5 rotating annual cycles x 30 units = 150 lesson units
- **Total:** 420 canonical lesson units before optional seasonal and supplemental material

## 3. Adjudication Method

Each pair was assessed against seven criteria:

1. **Fidelity to the supplied theological framework**
2. **Separation of biblical text, inference, framework position, and history**
3. **Developmental appropriateness**
4. **Actual spiral progression rather than nominal spiral language**
5. **Suitability for a mixed-age classroom rotation**
6. **Breadth and balance of biblical, doctrinal, formative, and apologetic coverage**
7. **Research quality and avoidance of unsupported certainty**

Agreement between the two systems was not treated as validation. The systems frequently rely on the same popular developmental theories, apologetics claims, and online ministry sources. Correlated model agreement can reproduce the same error twice.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Findings

### 4.1 The strongest shared insight is correct

All six files correctly reject disconnected moral stories and seek to place individual passages inside the larger biblical story. They also agree that content must progress from concrete narrative toward interpretation, doctrine, ethical reasoning, and independent study.

This principle survives intact:

> Every lesson must teach the passage faithfully, locate it within the whole biblical story, and form an age-appropriate response without reducing the passage to generic moral advice.

### 4.2 The proposed curricula are mostly chronological surveys, not true spirals

The documents repeatedly call their architecture a spiral, but most divide the canon into one large era per year:

- Kids A: Genesis, then Exodus/Judges, then Kings/Prophets, then Gospels, then Acts/church.
- Youths A and B: Creation to kingdom, then exile/prophets, then Gospels, then Acts/Epistles/Revelation.
- Teens A: Creation/Exodus, then kingdom/prophets, then Gospels, then cross/Acts, then epistles/history/worldviews.
- Teens B: Old Testament, then Jesus, then church, then worldview/ethics, then church history/eschatology.

That is a coherent multi-year survey, but it is not rotation-safe and it does not revisit the entire metanarrative annually with increasing depth.

### 4.3 All six files assume that curriculum year equals student age

The documents use formulations such as:

- Kids Year 5 as the material an eight-year-old receives after completing Years 1–4.
- Youth Years 1–2 for concrete thinkers and Years 3–4 for emerging abstract thinkers.
- Teen Year 1 at approximately age 13, progressing to Year 5 at approximately age 17.

That architecture only works when each age has its own cohort and progresses together every year. It fails if the church has one Kids class, one Youths class, and one Teens class containing several ages, which is the product structure currently assumed.

A rotation-safe design is therefore required. A student must be able to join any annual cycle without waiting several years to encounter Jesus, the gospel, the church, or the biblical big picture.

### 4.4 The source quality is uneven

The files contain useful citations, but several rely heavily on:

- ministry blogs;
- denominational advocacy pages;
- Wikipedia;
- Reddit;
- popular apologetics summaries;
- non-primary reviews of books rather than the books or underlying research;
- developmental stage models presented more confidently than current research warrants.

One Teen output provides no checkable bibliography at all, only a narrative description of source categories.

The lesson architecture can still be used. Specific factual, historical, linguistic, developmental, and apologetic claims must be independently checked during lesson production.

### 4.5 Several outputs overstate what evidence can establish

Examples include claims that:

- non-Christian sources prove Jesus' existence "irrefutably";
- predictive prophecy is statistically impossible by chance;
- the Roman tomb was absolutely secure;
- the early church unanimously rejected instruments;
- New Testament manuscripts were copied in a way that means modern translations can be trusted without qualification.

The canonical curriculum must not rely on these formulations. It should make the strongest claim the evidence supports, no stronger.

### 4.6 Several doctrinal positions were inserted without being supplied

The framework supplied by the project owner is specific on baptism, the Lord's Supper, a cappella worship, male eldership/assembly preaching, Scripture, canon, and church continuity. It did **not** settle all of the following:

- cessationism versus continuationism;
- libertarian free will versus other non-Calvinist accounts of freedom;
- a specific millennial system;
- a single theory of the atonement;
- inherited guilt/original sin in all its formulations;
- eternal conscious torment versus conditional immortality;
- pacifism versus just-war reasoning;
- a particular creation chronology;
- every divorce and remarriage case.

These cannot be silently encoded as project doctrine. They belong in adult review until explicitly decided.

### 4.7 The curriculum volume and the teaching calendar need reconciliation

The research brief requested 30–40 lessons per year, while the church calendar was earlier described as roughly 20–30 Sunday School meetings because of school holidays.

The proposed resolution is:

- Author **30 publishable units** per annual cycle.
- Mark **24–28 as core scheduled units**.
- Mark **2–6 as flex units** for review, catch-up, holidays, special Sundays, or family-only use.
- Allow the operating calendar to select which units are taught in a specific year without changing the canonical curriculum.

This separates curriculum completeness from the practical school-year schedule.

## 5. Pairwise Adjudication

## 5.1 Kids Ages 4–8

### Preferred base: K-A

K-A is the stronger base because it consistently recognises that ages 4–8 are concrete thinkers, that metaphor and typology are developmentally difficult, and that detailed theology should be deferred. It defines realistic outcomes in concrete language and treats baptism as a concept to understand without pressure.

### Material to retain from K-A

- Concrete-before-abstract pedagogy.
- Narrative coherence rather than moral fragmentation.
- Explicit distinction between text, inference, and tradition.
- The prohibition against premature baptismal pressure.
- A whole-story progression from Creation to church.
- The idea that technical theological vocabulary must remain tethered to narrative.
- Age-sensitive handling of death, judgment, violence, and traumatic passages.

### Material to salvage from K-B

- The five thematic lenses: relational care, promise, kingdom, discipleship, and redemptive fulfilment.
- Strong emphasis on wonder, prayer, song, participation, and belonging.
- Useful thematic lesson ideas around God's care, promise keeping, kingship, faithful response, and the church.
- The warning against phrases that children may literalise, such as an unexplained "Jesus lives in your heart."

### Material to reject or rewrite from K-B

K-B's Year 5 expects an eight-year-old to:

- master the mechanics of salvation;
- map types and antitypes;
- understand the purpose and context of epistles;
- study church organisation;
- analyse atonement and New Covenant fulfilment at a relatively formal level.

That is too much abstraction for the segment and contradicts the same document's developmental cautions. The problem is not that the doctrines are unimportant. The problem is the proposed cognitive depth and precision at age eight.

K-B also uses overconfident language such as "aligns perfectly with the maturation of the child's brain" and makes historical claims such as unanimous early rejection of instruments without sufficient support.

### Canonical Kids rule

Kids content may introduce:

- God as Creator, good, holy, loving, faithful, and just;
- human disobedience and the need for rescue;
- promise, rescue, kingdom, Jesus, cross, resurrection, church, prayer, worship, service, and hope;
- baptism as the response of a person able to believe and repent;
- the congregation's practice of weekly communion and vocal singing.

Kids content must not require children to master:

- formal atonement theories;
- detailed covenant systems;
- Greek lexical arguments;
- church-office controversies;
- detailed gender-role arguments;
- sacramental debates;
- systematic eschatology;
- proof-oriented apologetics.

Those matters may appear in teacher notes, but not as the child's central learning burden.

## 5.2 Youths Ages 9–12

### Preferred base: Y-A

Y-A is the stronger base because it treats development as uneven, delays serious apologetics until the upper part of the segment, and repeatedly distinguishes text, inference, pattern, and tradition. It is more honest about uncertainty and more careful about what a 9–12-year-old can reliably abstract.

### Material to retain from Y-A

- Transition from story recall to narrative comprehension.
- Timelines, maps, comparisons, and concrete structures in the lower years.
- Context questions: who wrote, to whom, what genre, what comes before and after.
- Apologetics beginning lightly around ages 11–12 rather than dominating the entire segment.
- Explicit treatment of a cappella practice as a conclusion from the perceived New Testament pattern, not an unqualified direct command prohibiting instruments.
- Charitable introduction to denominational diversity and church history.
- Basic command/example/inference reasoning taught as a method to examine, not an infallible algorithm.

### Material to salvage from Y-B

- A consistent 35-topic annual scope that can be reduced into 30 canonical units.
- Useful coverage of prophets, exile, intertestamental history, Jesus, Acts, epistles, and new creation.
- Local awareness that children may not receive ambient biblical literacy outside church.
- Strong motivation to connect doctrine to Scripture rather than teacher authority.
- Useful lesson concepts in archaeology, biblical transmission, and historical context, once rewritten with appropriate caution.

### Material to reject or rewrite from Y-B

- "Irrefutable proof" language.
- Statistical-probability claims about predictive prophecy unless supported by a defined, defensible methodology.
- Claims of absolute tomb security.
- Claims that manuscript copying guarantees unrestricted confidence in every modern translation.
- Simplistic accounts of a universal early baptismal practice or a single identifiable historical "departure."
- Apologetics beginning as a formal programme at age nine.

### Canonical Youths rule

The lower end of the segment should use concrete tools:

- timelines;
- maps;
- parallel-text comparison;
- cause and effect;
- genre identification;
- direct statement versus inference.

The upper end may begin:

- promise and fulfilment;
- basic typology with explicit textual controls;
- basic historical evidence;
- introductory apologetics;
- a fair statement of alternative Christian interpretations;
- structured moral reasoning.

No student should be pressured toward baptism. By the end of the segment, a student should be able to explain what baptism means, why the congregation teaches immersion as integral to conversion, and what discipleship entails.

## 5.3 Teens Ages 13–17

### Preferred epistemic base: T-A

T-A is stronger in intellectual posture. It explicitly requires students to say when evidence does not settle a question, to distinguish text from inference, and to engage serious alternatives. It treats doubt and disagreement as planned competencies rather than emergencies.

### Material to retain from T-A

- Conviction grounded in demonstrated reasoning from Scripture rather than inherited assertion.
- Hermeneutics and apologetics as threads across the whole segment.
- Developmental caution: formal reasoning varies and stage theories are not deterministic.
- Strong graduation outcomes in biblical literacy, interpretation, ethics, and independent study.
- Fair comparison of disputed interpretations.
- CEI/command-example-inference taught with both its usefulness and limitations.
- Separation of direct command, moral inference, and prudential judgment.

### Material to salvage from T-B

T-B is the stronger coverage inventory. Its useful material includes:

- sexuality, marriage, pornography, gender, abortion/euthanasia, race, wealth, politics, nationalism, war, environment, technology/AI, mental wellbeing, addiction, other religions, and pluralism;
- church history from the post-apostolic era through modern Churches of Christ;
- translation philosophy and study tools;
- capstone preparation for choosing a congregation, vocation, relationships, and independent discipleship;
- explicit rejection of weak arguments such as "silence always prohibits," "faith is blind," or "all denominations are evil."

### Material to reject or route to adult review from T-B

T-B silently defines the framework as:

- cessationist;
- libertarian-free-will;
- amillennial;
- opposed to inherited guilt using a "tabula rasa" formulation.

These were not supplied as project commitments and must not be encoded as settled doctrine.

Some T-B wording also embeds ideological conclusions before study, for example framing particular views as "unprovable," "undeniable," or equivalent to secular ideology. Teen lessons should state the Christian conclusion clearly but still represent the live question and strongest contrary reasoning accurately.

### Canonical Teens rule

Teen content should not be neutral in the sense of refusing to reach conclusions. It should be objective in method:

1. State the question.
2. Read the principal texts in context.
3. Separate observation from interpretation.
4. Present the framework-compatible conclusion.
5. State the strongest serious alternative.
6. Identify the interpretive hinge.
7. Mark what remains uncertain.
8. Apply the conclusion pastorally and ethically.

## 6. Canonical Curriculum Operating Model

## 6.1 Segment rotations, not age-locked cohorts

Each segment has an annual rotation. The whole class studies the same annual cycle, with differentiated prompts and activities inside each lesson.

This design works whether the church has:

- one mixed-age class per segment; or
- multiple classes within a segment.

An age-locked sequence does not work in the first scenario. A rotation-safe sequence works in both.

## 6.2 Every annual cycle contains the full gospel spine

No student should wait three or four years to encounter the cross, resurrection, church, or Christian hope.

Each annual cycle therefore contains six recurring anchors, taught through the year's theme rather than repeated verbatim:

1. God creates and rules.
2. Humanity rebels and needs rescue.
3. God promises and acts through the biblical story.
4. Jesus is Lord; He died and rose.
5. People respond to the gospel and live as the church.
6. God will complete His work in resurrection and new creation.

The remaining units explore the annual theme in depth.

## 6.3 Annual unit structure

Each annual cycle contains 30 publishable units:

- **24 thematic core units**
- **3 synthesis/review units**
- **3 flex units** for holidays, special events, catch-up, family-only study, or local pastoral needs

The operating calendar may schedule 24–28 of these as Sunday School lessons.

## 6.4 Differentiation inside a lesson

Each lesson has one shared passage and big idea, plus two delivery layers:

- **Foundation layer:** suitable for the younger or less experienced student in the segment.
- **Extension layer:** suitable for the older or more experienced student in the segment.

Examples:

- Kids 4–5: retell, identify, imitate, pray, draw, order pictures.
- Kids 6–8: explain cause/effect, compare, locate on timeline, use simple theological vocabulary.
- Youths 9–10: sequence, classify, locate context, identify direct statements.
- Youths 11–12: compare interpretations, infer carefully, answer introductory apologetic questions.
- Teens 13–15: establish argument structure and context.
- Teens 16–17: compare positions, examine evidence, apply ethically, lead discussion, complete independent study.

## 7. Proposed Canonical Annual Cycles

These are working titles and organizing emphases. They are not yet the final 420-lesson map.

## 7.1 Kids: Five-Cycle Rotation

### K1 — God Made and Cares

**Emphasis:** Creation, God's goodness, human value, fall, God's care, Jesus' compassion, prayer, church belonging.

**Required annual anchors:** Creation; fall; first promise; Jesus as Son and Lord; cross/resurrection; church family; new creation.

### K2 — God Keeps His Promises

**Emphasis:** Abraham, Joseph, Moses, David, prophets, Jesus as fulfilment, trustworthy speech, patience, hope.

**Required annual anchors:** Creation and promise; human failure; covenant stories; Jesus fulfils God's rescue promise; gospel response; future hope.

### K3 — God Rescues

**Emphasis:** Exodus, wilderness, judges, exile, healing and restoration, Jesus' saving work, repentance, baptism introduced without pressure.

**Required annual anchors:** Need for rescue; Passover/Exodus; repeated human failure; Jesus' death and resurrection; Acts conversion narratives; rescue completed in new creation.

### K4 — Jesus Is King

**Emphasis:** Israel's kings and prophets, kingdom expectations, Jesus' authority, kingdom parables, cross as self-giving kingship, church as the King's people.

**Required annual anchors:** God as King; failed human rule; promise to David; Jesus' kingdom; resurrection and Great Commission; faithful kingdom living.

### K5 — God's Family Lives the Story

**Emphasis:** Acts, church, prayer, teaching, sharing, service, weekly remembrance, congregational singing, giving, mission, faithful living.

**Required annual anchors:** Big-story review; Jesus' death/resurrection; Pentecost; gospel response; church practices; return of Christ and new creation.

## 7.2 Youths: Four-Cycle Rotation

### Y1 — One Bible, One Story

**Emphasis:** Bible structure, whole-story timeline, creation, fall, promise, covenant, Jesus, church, new creation; foundational interpretation habits.

### Y2 — Covenant, Kingdom, Wisdom, and Prophets

**Emphasis:** Israel's story, law, kings, exile, wisdom literature, prophetic justice and hope, promise/fulfilment controls, lament, faithful living.

### Y3 — Jesus, Gospel, and Discipleship

**Emphasis:** four Gospels, Jesus' identity, kingdom, parables, cross, resurrection, conversion, baptism, discipleship, evidence for Jesus and resurrection at an introductory level.

### Y4 — Church, Scripture, and Christian Hope

**Emphasis:** Acts, epistles, church life, Lord's Supper, singing, leadership, men and women serving, mission, denominational diversity, suffering, Revelation/new creation.

Each Youth cycle still includes the six annual anchors and a short whole-story synthesis.

## 7.3 Teens: Five-Cycle Rotation

### T1 — Scripture and the Big Story

**Emphasis:** canon, transmission, translation, genre, context, metanarrative, covenants, Israel, responsible use of extra-canonical context, basic study method.

### T2 — Jesus, Gospel, and Salvation

**Emphasis:** historical Jesus, Gospel portraits, kingdom, cross, resurrection, grace, faith, repentance, confession, immersion, discipleship, perseverance, major atonement images.

### T3 — Church, Worship, and Mission

**Emphasis:** Acts, church identity, Lord's Supper, vocal congregational worship, leadership, men and women, unity, discipline, mission, gifts, service, Christian community.

### T4 — Faith Under Pressure

**Emphasis:** apologetics, doubt, suffering, science/naturalism, sexuality, gender, marriage, pornography, life ethics, race, wealth, politics, nationalism, war, technology/AI, mental wellbeing, addiction, other religions.

### T5 — History, Hope, and Independent Faith

**Emphasis:** AD 33 to today, early Christian writings, councils as history rather than authority, institutional development, Reformation, Radical Reformation, Restoration history, Churches of Christ, eschatological views, study tools, vocation, choosing a congregation, capstone study.

Each Teen cycle still includes the six annual anchors so that a thirteen-year-old entering during T4 or T5 receives the gospel and metanarrative foundations that year.

## 8. Theological and Epistemic Classification

The CMS must not store doctrinal prose as an undifferentiated block. Every material doctrinal claim should carry a classification.

## 8.1 Claim classifications

### `explicit_text`

The canonical passage states the proposition directly or nearly directly.

Example: Jesus was raised from the dead.

### `canonical_synthesis`

The claim is built from several clear canonical texts rather than one isolated sentence.

Example: the overall Creation-Fall-Promise-Christ-Church-New Creation metanarrative.

### `strong_inference`

The conclusion is not directly stated in one sentence but is judged to follow strongly from canonical evidence.

Example: weekly observance inferred from the first-day gathering pattern and the role of the Supper.

### `framework_position`

The project adopts this conclusion after evaluating the relevant texts, while acknowledging that serious Christians interpret the evidence differently.

Examples: a cappella congregational worship as the authorised positive pattern; male eldership; baptism as integral to conversion.

### `disputed`

The project has not settled the issue, or the lesson intentionally compares serious interpretations.

Examples: cessationism, millennium, creation chronology, hell models.

### `historical_context`

The claim describes history, culture, language, textual transmission, or post-canonical Christian development. It informs interpretation but does not establish doctrine independently.

## 8.2 Doctrine scopes

### `primary`

Core material that belongs in the main pathway and can be taught positively at every segment with age-appropriate depth.

### `secondary_reviewed`

The project has adopted a position, but the issue requires explicit textual reasoning and acknowledgement of alternatives, especially for Teens.

### `adult_review_pending`

The research files raised the issue, but the project owner has not yet adopted a position.

### `pastoral_sensitive`

The subject may be biblically important but requires age, family, trauma, privacy, or pastoral safeguards.

## 8.3 Segment presentation rules

### Kids

- Teach primary content positively and concretely.
- Do not put denominational debates in child-facing material.
- Put necessary nuance in teacher notes.
- Avoid pressure, fear tactics, and false certainty.

### Youths

- Teach the congregation's reasoning.
- Briefly acknowledge that Christians differ where material.
- Introduce text versus inference.
- Reserve detailed polemics for later.

### Teens

- Teach the framework conclusion clearly.
- Present the strongest serious alternative.
- Identify the interpretive hinge.
- Mark uncertainty explicitly.
- Distinguish doctrine, moral inference, prudence, and politics.

## 9. Adult-Review Register

The following issues should not be treated as settled merely because one research model asserted a conclusion:

1. Formal terminology for Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, including how later Trinitarian language will be used without making a creed the authority.
2. Whether one atonement model is primary, or whether the curriculum presents several canonical images without forcing exclusivity.
3. Original sin, inherited guilt, inherited consequences, and personal accountability.
4. Cessationism and continuationism.
5. Calvinism, Arminianism, corporate election, and models of human freedom.
6. Perseverance, apostasy, and assurance.
7. Eternal conscious torment, conditional immortality, and other judgment interpretations.
8. Millennial and broader eschatological systems.
9. Creation chronology and evolution.
10. Pacifism, non-resistance, and just-war reasoning.
11. Divorce and remarriage cases.
12. Age of moral accountability and how readiness for baptism is assessed pastorally.
13. The exact historical claims the curriculum will make about continuity, apostasy, restoration, and Churches of Christ identity.

These issues should have dedicated adult decision records before their final Teen lessons are approved.

## 11. Editorial Workflow

The content system should reflect the following workflow:

1. **Canonical map approved**  
   Lesson title, passage, big idea, cycle, sequence, and outcomes are locked.

2. **Lesson brief created**  
   Exegetical question, developmental limits, doctrinal classifications, and required resources are specified.

3. **Draft produced**  
   AI may assist, but the draft is not publishable.

4. **Exegetical/theological review**  
   Review checks passage context, claims, framework fidelity, alternative views, and overstatement.

5. **Developmental review**  
   Review checks abstraction, language, activity load, emotional safety, and age differentiation.

6. **Teacher usability review**  
   A volunteer teacher must be able to use the zero-preparation card in real time.

7. **Family usability review**  
   The family experience must fit the stated time and require no specialist knowledge.

8. **Copy and accessibility review**  
   Plain language, reading level, screen-reader structure, printability, and mobile use are checked.

9. **Approved and published**  
   Only approved content is returned by the public API.

10. **Post-use revision**  
    Teacher/parent feedback creates a new version; published history remains auditable.

Recommended status values:

```text
draft
research_review
exegetical_review
developmental_review
usability_review
approved
scheduled
published
retired
```

## 12. Runtime AI Policy

For the initial system:

- Do not let a live model generate doctrine, answers, prayers, or child-facing advice on demand.
- Do not expose a children's chatbot.
- Use AI during controlled authoring and review, outside the public experience.
- Store model name, prompt version, generated draft, reviewer changes, and final approved version when AI materially contributes.
- The web app and future native clients should retrieve only approved CMS content.

This materially lowers theological, safeguarding, privacy, and consistency risk.

## 18. Final Adjudication Summary

The double pass succeeded in producing breadth and exposing alternatives, but it did not produce a ready curriculum. The most useful outcome is not either model's lesson sequence. It is the combination of:

- K-A's developmental restraint;
- Y-A's interpretive discipline;
- T-A's epistemic honesty;
- K-B's thematic imagination;
- Y-B's broad canonical inventory;
- T-B's comprehensive ethics and church-history inventory.

The canonical system must discard their shared weaknesses:

- nominal rather than actual spiralling;
- age-locked cohorts;
- weak source hierarchies;
- apologetic overclaiming;
- doctrinal positions invented beyond the supplied framework;
- insufficient distinction between curriculum content and historical research.

The project is now ready to move from six AI proposals to one governed curriculum architecture.
